### Logical Operators Definitions

*   **Negation ($\neg$)**: def. the negation of $p$ (not $p$).
*   **Conjunction ($\land$)**: $p$ and $q$.
    *   True only when both $p$ and $q$ is true.
*   **Disjunction ($\lor$)**: $p$ or $q$.
    *   **inclusive or**: True if $p$ is true, or $q$ is true or both.
*   **Exclusive or ($\oplus$)**:
    *   $p \oplus q$ is true when exactly one of $p$ or $q$ is true.
*   **The conditional Statement ($\to$)**:
    *   $p \to q$ is the statement if $p$ then $q$.
    *   $p$ is the **hypothesis**.
    *   $q$ is the **conclusion**.
    *   Only false when $p$ is true but $q$ is false.

#### Truth Table ($p \to q$)

| $p$ | $q$ | $p \to q$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | T |

**很重要的结论**
$$ p \to q \equiv (\neg p) \lor q $$

*   Common English phrasings of $p \to q$.
```
• “if p, then q” — If it is sunny, then I will go to the park. 
• “p implies q” — Being a square implies being a rectangle.
• “p only if q” — I will pass the exam only if I study hard. 
• “q whenever p” — The alarm sounds whenever smoke is detected.
• “q is necessary for p” — Being divisible by 4 is necessary for being divisible by 12. 
• “p is sufficient for q” — Scoring 90% is sufficient for getting an A.
• “q unless ¬p” — I will go to the party unless I am not healthy.
```

## Related Propositions

From the condition $p \to q$:

*   **Converse**: $q \to p$
*   **Inverse**: $\neg p \to \neg q$
*   **Contrapositive**: $\neg q \to \neg p$

> **Only $\neg q \to \neg p$ is equivalent to $p \to q$.**

**One finding**: a converse proposition is the contrapositive of the inverse proposition.
